Dot product as projection#

For unit vectors u^\hat{u}u^ and any vector vvv,

u^⋅v=∥v∥cos⁡θ\hat{u} \cdot v = \lVert v \rVert \cos\thetau^⋅v=∥v∥cosθ

is the (signed) length of vvv's shadow on u^\hat{u}u^. This single fact powers attention, kernels, and almost every similarity metric.

Matrix-vector product, two ways#

Given A∈Rm×nA \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}A∈Rm×n and x∈Rnx \in \mathbb{R}^nx∈Rn:

  • Row view: (Ax)i=ai⊤x(Ax)_i = a_i^\top x(Ax)i​=ai⊤​x — dot product of iii-th row with xxx.
  • Column view: Ax=∑jxj a:,jAx = \sum_j x_j \, a_{:,j}Ax=∑j​xj​a:,j​ — linear combination of AAA's columns weighted by xxx.

The column view is the one I reach for when reasoning about subspaces.

Eigen-decomposition one-liner#

If AAA is symmetric, A=QΛQ⊤A = Q \Lambda Q^\topA=QΛQ⊤ with orthonormal QQQ. Then Ak=QΛkQ⊤A^k = Q \Lambda^k Q^\topAk=QΛkQ⊤ — exponentiating a matrix reduces to exponentiating its eigenvalues.
