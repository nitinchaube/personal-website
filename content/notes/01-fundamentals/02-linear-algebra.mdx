## Dot product as projection

For unit vectors $\hat{u}$ and any vector $v$,

$$
\hat{u} \cdot v = \lVert v \rVert \cos\theta
$$

is the (signed) length of $v$'s shadow on $\hat{u}$. This single fact powers attention, kernels, and almost every similarity metric.

## Matrix-vector product, two ways

Given $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

- **Row view**: $(Ax)_i = a_i^\top x$ — dot product of $i$-th row with $x$.
- **Column view**: $Ax = \sum_j x_j \, a_{:,j}$ — linear combination of $A$'s columns weighted by $x$.

The column view is the one I reach for when reasoning about subspaces.

## Eigen-decomposition one-liner

If $A$ is symmetric, $A = Q \Lambda Q^\top$ with orthonormal $Q$. Then $A^k = Q \Lambda^k Q^\top$ — exponentiating a matrix reduces to exponentiating its eigenvalues.
